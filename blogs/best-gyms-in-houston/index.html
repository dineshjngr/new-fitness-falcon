The best gym in Houston depends on what you train and where you live. For cheap 24/7 access close to Downtown, EōS Fitness on Allen Parkway is the lowest-priced club we could verify, at $39.99 a month plus fees. For serious powerlifting and strongman, 21st Barbell in Timbergrove stands out. For a full-service premium club, Life Time GreenStreet (Downtown) and Equinox River Oaks lead. For coached HYROX, CrossFit-style and functional classes, look at Urban Athletics, Breach Fitness, HNS Training Facility and CrossFit Live Oak. Memorial Athletic Club in west Houston is a full-service club with a separate women’s facility.

Key takeaways

  • EōS Fitness on Allen Parkway is the cheapest verified 24/7 gym in central Houston at $39.99 a month, but its $59.99 annual fee, enrollment fee and tax add to the real cost.
  • 21st Barbell in Timbergrove is the standout for powerlifting and strongman, with monolifts, specialty bars, 24/7 code entry and a $20 day pass.
  • Life Time GreenStreet publishes premium memberships from $219 a month, while Equinox River Oaks does not publish prices; Memorial Athletic Club adds a pool and a separate women's facility in west Houston.
  • Texas law lets you cancel a new gym contract for a full refund within three business days by certified mail, and caps contract terms at three years.

How this list was made: The Fitness Falcon did not visit, tour or train at any of these gyms. This is a research-based guide. Every gym was checked on its official website and at least one independent source on 13 October 2026. No gym paid to be included. Prices are exactly as each gym publishes them. If a gym doesn’t publish a price, we say so. If you are also comparing national chains, see our guide to US gym chain membership prices and fees.

What gym memberships cost in Houston

Among the gyms we could verify, published monthly prices run from $39.99 (EōS) to $249 (Life Time City Centre). That headline number is only part of the cost:

  • Annual fees. EōS charges $59.99 a year on top of monthly dues.
  • Enrollment or initiation fees. EōS lists up to $49.99. Equinox initiation is reported at $300 or more.
  • Payment method. EōS’s advertised rates assume bank autopay.
  • Notice periods. EōS and Life Time both ask for 30 days’ notice to cancel. CrossFit Live Oak asks for 15 days before the 1st.
  • Single visits. The cheapest verified day passes are 21st Barbell ($20) and HNS ($20 gym). Several gyms also offer free trials: EōS (7 days), HNS (3 days), Memorial Athletic Club (1 day), and Urban Athletics and 21st Barbell (one session or visit).

Planet Fitness, the usual answer to "cheapest gym," has many Houston clubs, but its club pages blocked our automated checks, so it isn’t listed here. Our US gym chain price comparison covers Planet Fitness and other national chains, including their annual fees.

How to choose a gym in Houston

  1. Start with your schedule. If you train before 5am or late at night, only EōS and 21st Barbell on this list are open 24/7. Class gyms like Urban Athletics and CrossFit Live Oak work only if their class times fit your day.
  2. Pick a gym near home or work. Houston is spread out and most people drive, so a gym on your commute is easier to stick with than a better one across town. This is general advice, not a measured finding.
  3. Ask about parking. None of the gyms here publish parking details on the pages we checked. This matters most Downtown, where garage parking may cost extra, so ask before you join.
  4. Think about summer. Houston summers are hot and humid. A local guide notes August heat indexes around 105°F and advises training outdoors only around sunrise or after dark between May and September. The CDC’s heat and health guidance explains who is most at risk. An air-conditioned gym makes summer training more consistent. 21st Barbell, for example, advertises A/C and an ice machine.
  5. Price the whole first year. Add enrollment, annual fees, taxes and any add-ons to 12 months of dues before comparing gyms.
  6. Use the trial. Try the gym at the time of day you’d actually go, so you see how busy it is. If you’ve been away for a while, our guide to returning to the gym after a long break has a sensible first-month plan.
  7. Read the cancellation terms before you sign. Check the notice period and how you have to cancel (online, in person or by letter).

Texas gym contract basics

Texas regulates gym and health club contracts under the Health Spa Act, Chapter 702 of the Texas Occupations Code. The main points for members:

  • Three-business-day cancellation. You can cancel a new contract for a full refund if you send notice by midnight of the third business day after the contract date. The notice must be in writing, include proof of payment, and go by certified mail to the gym’s home office. The refund is due within 30 days (§702.307).
  • Maximum term. A contract can’t run longer than three years. If it’s financed through a retail installment contract, payments can’t run longer than five years (§702.303).
  • Other cancellation rights. You may cancel and get a refund of unearned payments in these cases (§702.308):
    • the gym closes and doesn’t provide another facility within 10 miles;
    • it moves more than 10 miles away;
    • it fails to provide the services it advertised;
    • the member dies or becomes totally and permanently disabled.
  • Moving away isn’t on the list. Unlike some states, Texas doesn’t give you a statutory right to cancel because you move. That depends on your contract.

Many Houston gyms sell month-to-month memberships with their own notice periods, which are separate from these statutory rights. Keep a copy of your contract and any cancellation letter. For more on federal and state cancellation rules, see our guide to gym fees and how cancellation works.

This is general information, not legal advice. For a specific dispute, read your contract and contact the Texas Attorney General’s consumer protection division or a lawyer.

How we chose these gyms

We built this list from desk research, not visits. We didn’t tour, train at or get paid by any of these gyms, and none paid to be included. We kept only gyms whose current details we could confirm on the gym’s own website and at least one independent source, such as a local directory listing, a property directory, trade press or a published local guide. We aimed for a mix of budgets and training styles. For each gym we recorded what it publishes: address, hours, equipment and classes, membership and day-pass prices, and the fees that change the real monthly cost. Where a gym doesn’t publish a price, we say so instead of guessing. We don’t use star ratings or review counts. Everything was checked on 13 October 2026. Prices and hours change often, so confirm them with the gym before you join.

Some well-known options are missing because we couldn’t verify them in time. Planet Fitness and YMCA of Greater Houston both blocked our automated checks. We’ll add them once we can confirm details from the official sources.

How we score these gyms

Each gym gets a score from 1 to 5 on five criteria, in half-point steps. The overall score is the average of the five, rounded to one decimal place. We score only what each gym publishes, as described in this guide, and we checked it all on 13 October 2026. We did not visit, tour or train at any of these gyms, and no gym paid for its score.

When a gym doesn't publish its price, its value score is capped at 3.0, because you can't judge value without a price. A specialist gym can still score highly on facilities if its equipment is strong for what it does. These scores are a starting point for your shortlist. They are not a substitute for a trial visit at the time you'd actually train.

  • Value: the price compared with what's included, after enrollment, annual and processing fees.
  • Facilities: the equipment and amenities listed, such as platforms, pools, saunas, recovery areas and studios.
  • Access: opening hours (24/7 scores highest), day passes or free trials, and the number of locations.
  • Coaching: the range of classes, coaching and programming on offer.
  • Transparency: whether prices, contract and cancellation terms, and hours are clearly published.

FAQ

What is the cheapest gym in Houston?

Of the gyms we verified, EōS Fitness is the cheapest monthly option at $39.99, but you also pay a $59.99 annual fee, enrollment and tax. For single visits, 21st Barbell’s $20 day pass and HNS’s $20 gym drop-in are the cheapest published options. Budget chains like Planet Fitness may cost less but weren’t verified for this list.

Which Houston gyms are open 24 hours?

On this list, EōS Fitness (Allen Parkway and W Gray St) and 21st Barbell (code entry) are open 24/7.

Can I buy a gym day pass in Houston?

Yes. 21st Barbell charges $20, HNS charges $20 for the gym or $30 for a class, Urban Athletics charges $30 per class, and Breach charges $25 for your first class. Life Time sells day passes but doesn’t publish the price. EōS and Memorial Athletic Club offer free passes to first-time local visitors.

What is the best gym in Houston for powerlifting?

21st Barbell. Its published equipment list includes monolifts, competition benches, a deep collection of specialty bars and strongman equipment, and it’s open 24/7.

Where can I train for HYROX in Houston?

HNS Training Facility and Urban Athletics both describe themselves as HYROX affiliates on their websites. Breach Fitness also runs HYROX classes.

Is there a women-only gym in Houston?

Memorial Athletic Club runs a separate facility called MAC for Women at 14633 Memorial Drive, which Houstonia has described as women-only. Ask the club which memberships include it. Equinox River Oaks isn’t women-only, but its Pilates, barre and yoga studios may suit people who prefer studio classes.

How much does a luxury gym cost in Houston?

Life Time publishes adult memberships from $219 a month (GreenStreet) and $249 (City Centre). Equinox River Oaks doesn’t publish prices. A third-party guide reported $215 to $405 a month plus an initiation fee.

Can I cancel a gym contract in Texas?

Yes, within three business days of signing, by certified mail, for a full refund. You can also cancel later if the gym closes or moves more than 10 miles, or fails to provide advertised services. After that, your contract’s own terms apply.
