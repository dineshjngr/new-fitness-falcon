The best gym in New York City depends on whether you want a pool, a serious lifting floor, or simply the most clubs for your money. For a big-box membership with Olympic platforms and classes, Crunch's Signature club at Union Square publishes $114.75 a month for one club or $127.20 for all of them. For a saltwater pool at a mid-range price, CompleteBody Midtown East is $120 a month on a Passport membership. For a family-owned lifting gym with honest, published rates, GYM NYC runs four locations from $125 a month. For full-service clubs with indoor and outdoor pools, Mercedes Club in Hell's Kitchen and Chelsea Piers Fitness on Pier 60 lead, though neither publishes a price. The McBurney YMCA on West 14th Street is the community option with a pool, a track and financial assistance.

Key takeaways

  • CompleteBody's Passport membership is the lowest published rate we verified in Manhattan, at $104 a month on 12 months or $120 month-to-month, and it includes a saltwater pool.
  • Crunch's Signature club at Union Square publishes $114.75 for one club or $127.20 for all clubs, with Olympic platforms and classes included.
  • GYM NYC publishes every rate for its four lifting gyms, from $125 a month plus initiation, with $35 to $40 day passes.
  • Mercedes Club and Chelsea Piers have the best pools and amenities but don't publish prices; New York law lets you cancel a new contract within three business days by mail or email.

How this list was made: The Fitness Falcon did not visit, tour or train at any of these gyms. This is a research-based guide, rewritten in October 2026 to replace an earlier version of this page. Every gym was checked on its official website and at least one independent listing on 18 October 2026. No gym paid to be included. Prices are exactly as each gym publishes them, and where a gym doesn't publish a price we say so. We don't quote star ratings or review counts. What gym memberships cost in New York City

Among the gyms we could verify, published monthly prices run from $104 (CompleteBody Passport on a 12-month commitment) to $204 (CompleteBody Premium month-to-month). Three of the six gyms, including both luxury clubs, don't publish a price. The headline number is only part of the cost:

  • Initiation fees. GYM NYC charges $150 to $200 on month-to-month plans and nothing on prepaid terms. Crunch doesn't show its enrollment fee on the club page.
  • Commitment. CompleteBody's cheapest rates need 12 months or a year paid up front. GYM NYC's prepaid terms are non-refundable by nature, so be sure before you buy a year.
  • Day passes. GYM NYC publishes $35 to $40. CompleteBody offers a free first visit. Crunch offers a free trial. Mercedes Club, Chelsea Piers and the Y don't publish one.
  • Budget chains. Blink Fitness and Planet Fitness have many New York clubs at lower prices, but neither could be verified from an official page for this guide. Our US gym chain price comparison covers their published rates and annual fees.

How to choose a gym in New York City

  1. Pick a gym on your commute. None of these clubs is open 24/7, so the one you pass twice a day wins. The picks cover Union Square, Midtown East, Nolita, the East Village, Downtown Brooklyn, Murray Hill, Hell's Kitchen, Chelsea and West 14th Street.
  2. Decide whether you need a pool. Four gyms here have one: CompleteBody Midtown East, Mercedes Club, Chelsea Piers and the McBurney Y. Only CompleteBody publishes its price.
  3. Price the whole first year. Add initiation or enrollment fees to 12 months of dues before comparing. A $150 initiation fee on a $125 membership adds $12.50 a month over a year.
  4. Use the trial at the hour you'd train. Crunch's free trial and CompleteBody's free day pass let you see how busy the floor is at your time. If you've been away a while, our guide to returning to the gym after a long break has a sensible first-month plan.
  5. Read the cancellation terms. New York law gives you specific rights, below, but your contract's notice period still applies.

New York gym contract basics

New York regulates health club contracts under Article 30 of the General Business Law. Section 624, in the version on the State Senate's website as of 2025, gives members these rights:

  • Three-business-day cancellation. You can cancel within three business days of receiving a copy of the written contract, by written notice sent by mail or email to the address in the contract. Refunds are due within ten business days.
  • Later cancellation. You can cancel if you become significantly physically disabled for more than three months (with a doctor's order), if you move more than 25 miles from any club the seller operates, or if the club permanently discontinues or substantially changes its services. A member's estate can cancel on death.
  • How you can cancel. Clubs must accept cancellation by website, email, phone, mail or in person, and must accept it through their website if you signed up there.
  • Renewals. An annual membership can be cancelled within 15 business days after a renewal takes effect, and a monthly one within three business days.

For federal rules and other states, see our guide to gym fees and how cancellation works.

This is general information, not legal advice. For a specific dispute, read your contract and contact the New York Attorney General's consumer protection bureau or a lawyer.

How we chose these gyms

We built this list from desk research, not visits. We didn't tour, train at or get paid by any of these gyms, and none paid to be included. We started from the gyms in the earlier version of this page and well-known New York clubs, then kept only those whose current details we could confirm on the gym's own website and at least one independent listing. We aimed for a spread of budgets and training styles: a big-box chain club, a mid-range pool club, a lifting gym, two full-service clubs and a community Y. For each gym we recorded what it publishes: address, hours, equipment and classes, membership and day-pass prices, and the fees that change the real monthly cost. Where a gym doesn't publish a price, we say so instead of guessing. We don't use star ratings or review counts. Everything was checked on 18 October 2026.

How we score these gyms

Each gym gets a score from 1 to 5 on five criteria, in half-point steps. The overall score is the average of the five, rounded to one decimal place. We score only what each gym publishes, as described in this guide. We did not visit, tour or train at any of these gyms, and no gym paid for its score.

When a gym doesn't publish its price, its value score is capped at 3.0, because you can't judge value without a price. A specialist gym can still score highly on facilities if its equipment is strong for what it does. These scores are a starting point for your shortlist. They are not a substitute for a trial visit at the time you'd actually train.

  • Value: the price compared with what's included, after initiation, annual and processing fees.
  • Facilities: the equipment and amenities listed, such as platforms, pools, saunas, recovery areas and studios.
  • Access: opening hours (24/7 scores highest), day passes or free trials, and the number of locations.
  • Coaching: the range of classes, coaching and programming on offer.
  • Transparency: whether prices, contract and cancellation terms, and hours are clearly published.

FAQ

What is the cheapest gym in New York City?

Of the gyms we verified, CompleteBody's Passport membership is the lowest published rate at $104 a month on a 12-month commitment, or $120 month-to-month. Budget chains such as Blink and Planet Fitness advertise less, but we couldn't verify their New York clubs for this guide.

Are there 24-hour gyms in Manhattan?

None of the gyms on this list is open 24 hours. The longest hours here are Crunch Union Square (5am–11pm Monday to Thursday) and GYM NYC's East 3rd, Brooklyn and Lexington locations (until 11pm Monday to Thursday).

Can I buy a gym day pass in NYC?

Yes. GYM NYC sells day passes for $35 (East 3rd and Brooklyn) or $40 (Mulberry and Lexington). CompleteBody offers a complimentary first day, and Crunch a free trial. Mercedes Club, Chelsea Piers and the McBurney Y don't publish a day pass.

Which NYC gyms have a pool?

CompleteBody Midtown East (saltwater), Mercedes Club (indoor and outdoor), Chelsea Piers Fitness (75-foot saltwater) and the McBurney YMCA.

How much does Chelsea Piers Fitness cost?

Chelsea Piers doesn't publish membership prices. You request them through an inquiry form or a tour.

Which is the best gym in NYC for lifting?

GYM NYC is the dedicated lifting gym on this list, with four locations and fully published rates. Crunch Union Square and Chelsea Piers both list Olympic platforms.

Can I cancel a gym contract in New York?

Yes. Within three business days of receiving your contract, by mail or email, for a full refund within ten business days. After that, you can cancel for a long-term disability, a move of more than 25 miles, or if the club discontinues its services. Clubs must accept cancellation online, by email, by phone, by mail or in person.
