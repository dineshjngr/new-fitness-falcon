Samsung Galaxy Watch 9 Review: Is It Worth It for Fitness, Running and Sleep?

A research-based Galaxy Watch 9 review: US prices, independent heart rate, GPS, sleep and battery tests, FDA-cleared features, and who should buy it.

The Samsung Galaxy Watch 9 is a strong health tracker for Android users, especially Samsung Galaxy phone owners, but it is only a modest fitness upgrade over the Galaxy Watch 8. Independent reviewers found accurate GPS, good sleep tracking and a useful new layer of coaching scores. They also found inconsistent workout heart rate readings and, above all, battery life that usually means charging every day.

Key takeaways

  • The Galaxy Watch 9 is a strong health tracker for Samsung Galaxy phone owners but only a modest fitness upgrade over the Galaxy Watch 8.
  • Reviewers found accurate GPS and good sleep tracking, but inconsistent workout heart rate at run starts and pace changes.
  • Battery life is the main weakness: reviewers mostly measured about 24–32 hours, so plan to charge daily.
  • It does not work with iPhone, and ECG, blood pressure and sleep apnea features require a Galaxy phone; blood pressure is not FDA-cleared.

How this review was made: The Fitness Falcon has not tested the Galaxy Watch 9. This is a research-based review. It combines Samsung’s official specifications and feature notes with published tests from Tom’s Guide, TechRadar, Android Central, Pickr, Android Authority, Wareable, Notebookcheck, Smartprix and AndroidPlanet. Every test result below is credited to the outlet that measured it. Prices and feature availability were checked on 12 October 2026.

Verdict at a glance

  • Buy it if you use a Samsung Galaxy phone, own a Galaxy Watch 6 or older, and want sleep, heart and body composition insights without a subscription.
  • Skip it if you own a Galaxy Watch 8, train seriously for races, use an iPhone, or want a watch that lasts several days.
  • Biggest strength: the widest health sensor set on a Wear OS watch, plus new scores that explain what the data means.
  • Biggest weakness: battery life. Reviewers measured roughly one to one-and-a-half days with typical use, and charging takes around 90 minutes.

Published scores so far range from 3.5/5 (Android Central) to 4/5 (TechRadar and Pickr), with Tom’s Guide at 3.75/5. Our research-based score, explained in the scorecard below, is 3.7/5.

Galaxy Watch 9 price in the US

Samsung announced the Galaxy Watch 9 on 22 July 2026, alongside the Galaxy Watch Ultra2. General availability began on 7 August. There are two sizes, 40mm and 44mm, each with Bluetooth or LTE versions. There is no Classic model with a rotating bezel this year.

The Watch 9 prices come from Samsung’s US store. Tom’s Guide notes a $30 increase over the Watch 8: the 40mm Bluetooth model was $349 last year, and the 44mm Bluetooth model was $379. Some early reports listed the larger model as 45mm at $429, but Samsung’s spec sheet and US store list it as 44mm at $409.99. LTE models need a carrier plan. In the UK, Samsung lists the watch from £349.

Prices change often. During October sales, the outgoing Galaxy Watch 8 has been widely discounted well below the Watch 9, and Samsung runs trade-in offers. Check current prices before you buy.

What’s new, what’s carried over, and what older watches get

Much of the Watch 9’s health software runs in Samsung Health and One UI 9 Watch, Samsung’s software layer on Wear OS 7. Some of these features will also reach older Galaxy watches. This matters more than the hardware changes when deciding whether to upgrade.

New hardware

  • Chip: Qualcomm Snapdragon Wear Elite (SDW6100), replacing Samsung’s Exynos W1000. Reviewers describe it as noticeably faster.
  • Battery: 390mAh in the 40mm model (up from 325mAh) and 445mAh in the 44mm model (up from 435mAh). Samsung’s rated battery life is still up to 30 hours (40mm) and 40 hours (44mm), the same as the Watch 8, according to Tom’s Guide.
  • Connectivity: Bluetooth 6.0 and dual-band Wi-Fi. GPS is still L1+L5 dual-frequency.
  • Unchanged: case design (8.6mm thick, 31.5g or 34g), sapphire crystal, 3,000-nit display, 5ATM + IP68 + MIL-STD-810H, and the Samsung BioActive Sensor (optical heart rate, ECG and bioelectrical impedance for body composition).

Is Fitness Index exclusive to the Watch 9? Tom’s Guide describes it as “for now, exclusive to the Galaxy Watch 9 and Galaxy Watch Ultra 2”. Samsung’s launch press release says it is “only available with Galaxy Watch7 or above with One UI 9 Watch or above.” Both statements are accurate. The feature depends on the software, not the watch hardware. As of 12 October 2026, One UI 9 Watch is still in beta for the Watch 8, Watch 7 and original Ultra (beta 4 arrived on the Watch 8 in early October, according to Sammy Fans). Samsung has not announced a stable release date. In practice, Fitness Index is exclusive to the new watches for now and should reach Watch 7 and later models when the stable update arrives.

Several features shown at launch belong only to the Galaxy Watch Ultra2. These include Trail Run mode, hydration alerts, the Mares dive app and the 5,000-nit display. Do not expect them on the Watch 9.

Fitness and running features

The Watch 9 adds coaching scores, but reviewers found them uneven.

  • Fitness Index uses your step average, VO2 max estimate, average heart rate, a body composition (BIA) reading and workout recovery to build a 0–100 score. It also gives sub-scores for body composition, cardio, endurance, strength and flexibility. You need at least one BIA and one VO2 max measurement and two days of activity before it works. Android Central’s Derrek Lee called it “one of the more confusing features”, because it is hard to know how to improve each sub-score.
  • Daily Cardio Load sets a daily training target based on time spent in heart rate zone 3 and above. It needs seven days of data, and Samsung recommends 28 days. TechRadar’s Tom Bedford noted that it counts only logged workouts, so “20,000 steps will count for less than a 100 meter run.” If walking is your main exercise, this score will undercount your effort. Our guide to Zone 2 cardio without a heart rate monitor explains how intensity zones work.
  • Running Coach launched with the Watch 8 and is still labelled beta. TechRadar says it produced “a 4-week training plan which I could complete in two days.” For a more established plan system, compare Garmin’s running plan features.
  • Workout detection and modes: Tom’s Guide found automatic workout detection worked well. TechRadar found it unreliable for city walking. Both noted missing modes, such as e-biking and skateboarding (Tom’s Guide) and bouldering (TechRadar).

Samsung Health does not charge a subscription for these insights. Tom’s Guide lists “no paywall for advanced holistic insights” as a reason to buy.

What this means: GPS looks reliable for road runs and walks. Optical heart rate was fine for steady efforts and everyday tracking. However, two outlets saw lag or overshoot at the start of runs and during changes in pace. If you do intervals, HIIT, or train by heart rate zone, pair the watch with a Bluetooth chest strap or consider a running-focused watch. Sleep tracking

The watch is light (31.5g for the 40mm and 34g for the 44mm) and thin. Reviewers consistently describe it as comfortable to wear overnight, which matters because many of its health scores rely on sleep data.

  • Tom’s Guide wore the Watch 9 and Pixel Watch 5 to bed for a week and found they produced “surprisingly similar sleep data”. It also noted that the sleep score and Energy Score carry over from the Watch 8.
  • TechRadar found the watch good at telling real sleep from lying awake in bed. However, it missed some night wakings and sometimes didn’t sync all sleep data. The reviewer said the Energy Score “felt light years from accurate”.
  • Smartprix listed excellent sleep tracking as a pro. AndroidPlanet found Bedtime Guidance useful day to day.

The new Vitals dashboard needs at least seven nights of sleep data before it can compare your overnight heart rate, HRV, breathing rate, skin temperature and SpO2 against your baseline. For context on what those overnight numbers can and cannot tell you, see our explainers on HRV for fitness and readiness scores.

Battery life and charging: the main weakness

Every review we found treats battery life as the Watch 9’s main problem. The larger battery capacity has not produced longer battery life in published tests.

Android Authority’s review also calls battery life the watch’s biggest weakness. It says the features are only useful if you charge every night. Android Authority separately reported that some Watch 9 and Ultra2 owners saw charging take two to four hours. This report is unconfirmed by Samsung and may be linked to heat or charger compatibility.

Practical impact: you need the watch on your wrist overnight for sleep, Vitals and Heart Health data, and on your wrist during workouts for training load. That makes daily charging awkward. Most owners will need a regular daytime top-up, such as during a shower or at a desk. If you dislike daily charging, a Garmin or a smart ring may suit you better. Health features and their limits

  • ECG (FDA-cleared): Samsung’s ECG Monitor App received FDA 510(k) clearance in August 2020. It runs through the Samsung Health Monitor app. TechRadar paired the watch with a non-Samsung phone and found “some features are gated for Galaxy device owners, like ECG or blood pressure.”
  • Sleep apnea (FDA-authorized): Samsung’s Sleep Apnea Feature received FDA De Novo authorization in February 2024. Samsung says the Watch 9 version, which sorts breathing disruptions into three levels, is “backed by new FDA clearance”. We could not find a separate FDA record for the upgrade, so that claim is Samsung’s. It requires a Galaxy phone running Android 12 or later and is for users aged 18 and over. It is not for people already diagnosed with sleep apnea or who are pregnant, and it does not replace diagnosis by a healthcare professional.
  • Blood pressure (US wellness feature, not FDA-cleared): Samsung enabled blood pressure tracking for US Galaxy Watch users in April 2026, according to T3. Samsung positions it as a wellness feature rather than a medical-grade tool. It is not cuffless on its own. You must calibrate it with a regular cuff, and recalibrate periodically.
  • Heart Health Score requires a Samsung Galaxy phone and 24 hours of initial wear. Android Central could not use it without a blood pressure cuff for calibration. Tom’s Guide says it needs several nights of data.
  • Missing safety tools: Tom’s Guide notes the Watch 9 lacks the Pixel Watch’s Loss of Pulse Detection and Breathing Emergency Detection.

Apart from the FDA-cleared ECG and sleep apnea features, these are wellness features. None of them is a diagnosis. An alert, or the absence of one, is not a clinical result. Speak to a doctor about symptoms or readings that concern you. For a similar look at how a ring maker gates health tools by country, see our Oura Health Radar availability guide.

Phone compatibility and smart features

The Galaxy Watch 9 needs an Android phone running Android 13 or later with Google Mobile Services. It does not work with iPhone. With a non-Samsung Android phone you still get workouts, sleep, GPS and Google Wallet. However, you lose ECG, blood pressure, sleep apnea detection, Heart Health Score and Samsung Pay (Pickr). On the smart side, Tom’s Guide called Gemini “easily the best smartwatch personal assistant” it has used, and the new raise-to-talk gesture makes it quicker to start.

Galaxy Watch 9 vs Galaxy Watch 8: should you upgrade?

For most Watch 8 owners, no. Tom’s Guide called the Watch 9 “basically a Galaxy Watch 8 with support for a few extra features”. Pickr said the Watch 8 “might just end up being better value”. Daily Cardio Load and Fitness Index are due to reach the Watch 8 through One UI 9 Watch, and the design, display and real-world battery life are essentially the same. What you would gain is a faster chip, Heart Health Score, raise-to-talk Gemini and a softer strap. Owners of a Watch 5, Watch 6 or older will notice a bigger difference, especially in speed and sensor features.

Who should buy and who should skip

Good fit

  • Samsung Galaxy phone owners upgrading from a Watch 6 or older
  • Everyday exercisers who want guidance on their data rather than raw numbers
  • People focused on sleep and general health who are happy to charge every day
  • Casual runners who mainly need accurate GPS distance

Probably not for you

  • Galaxy Watch 8 owners
  • Marathon, triathlon or interval-focused athletes who need dependable heart rate and multi-day battery
  • iPhone users
  • Non-Samsung Android users who want every health feature they pay for

Frequently asked questions

Is the Galaxy Watch 9 good for running?

It is good for casual and 5K–10K runners. In Android Central’s 5K test, it recorded the most accurate distance of the three watches tested. However, the same test showed heart rate lagging and overshooting early in the run. Serious runners should pair a chest strap or consider a Garmin.

How long does the Galaxy Watch 9 battery last?

Samsung rates the 40mm at up to 30 hours and the 44mm at up to 40 hours. Reviewers mostly measured about 24–32 hours, with AndroidPlanet reaching 1.5–2 days on lighter settings. Plan to charge daily. A full charge takes around 90 minutes.

Does the Galaxy Watch 9 work with iPhone?

No. It requires an Android 13 or later phone with Google services. ECG, blood pressure, sleep apnea detection and Heart Health Score also require a Samsung Galaxy phone.

Will the Galaxy Watch 8 get the Watch 9’s new features?

Partly. Samsung’s footnotes say Fitness Index and Daily Cardio Load work on Galaxy Watch 7 and newer running One UI 9 Watch. That update was still in beta on 12 October 2026. Heart Health Score and raise-to-talk Gemini are currently limited to the Watch 9 and Ultra2.

Is the Galaxy Watch 9 FDA-cleared?

The watch is not a medical device, but two of its features are FDA-regulated in the US. The ECG app has had 510(k) clearance since 2020, and the sleep apnea feature received De Novo authorization in 2024. Blood pressure tracking is offered in the US as a wellness feature, not an FDA-cleared one. All three require a Samsung Galaxy phone.

Does the Galaxy Watch 9 measure blood pressure in the US?

Yes, with conditions. US blood pressure tracking arrived in 2026 as a wellness feature. It needs a Galaxy phone and calibration against a regular cuff. It is an estimate, not a replacement for a validated cuff reading.
