In 2026, the cheapest national gym chains in the US advertise memberships from about $10 to $15 a month, mid-range chains usually cost about $25 to $50, and premium clubs such as Life Time and Equinox cost roughly $200 to $400 or more. The headline price is rarely what you pay. Enrollment fees, annual fees of $49 to $89 and processing charges can add $100 to $250 or more to your first year. Cancelling is also harder than joining at some chains: there is no federal "click-to-cancel" rule in force, so your rights depend on how you signed up and which state you live in.

Key takeaways

  • Budget US gym chains advertise memberships from about $10 to $15 a month, mid-range chains usually cost about $25 to $50, and premium clubs such as Life Time and Equinox cost roughly $200 to $400 or more.
  • Enrollment fees, annual fees of $49 to $89 and processing charges can make a cheap headline price cost more in year one, so compare the first-year total at the club you would join.
  • There is no federal click-to-cancel rule in force as of October 2026; ROSCA covers memberships sold online, and state laws such as those in Texas, Washington, California and Georgia add cancellation rights.
  • Before signing, use a trial pass at peak hours, get the cancellation method and notice period in writing, and check that trainers hold an NCCA-accredited certification.

For context, the Health & Fitness Association's 2024 consumer report put average monthly dues at US fitness facilities at $65 in 2023, as cited by NerdWallet. Most big-box chains advertise well below that, which is why fees matter so much in the comparison.

What the true first-year cost looks like

The simplest way to compare gyms is to add up the first year: enrollment fee + 12 months of dues + annual fee, plus any tax or processing fees. Two examples using the chains' own published figures:

  • Planet Fitness Classic: $0–$59 enrollment + $180 in dues ($15 × 12) + $49 annual fee = $229 to $288.
  • EōS Fitness at the advertised $9.99: up to $199.99 enrollment + $119.88 in dues + $59.99 annual fee = up to about $380.

The cheaper monthly price can end up costing more in year one. Club-level prices can also be well above the national "as low as" figure. For example, the two Houston Inner Loop EōS clubs we checked for our best gyms in Houston guide listed $39.99 a month for the entry tier. Always price the specific club you would join.

Gym fees and contracts explained

Most surprises come from terms, not the monthly price. Here is what to look for.

  • Enrollment or initiation fee: a one-time charge at sign-up. It ranges from $0 to $199.99 among the chains above and is often discounted during promotions.
  • Annual fee: a yearly charge on top of monthly dues ($49 at Planet Fitness, $59.99 at EōS, $69.99 at 24 Hour Fitness, up to $89 at Crunch). Ask when it is billed, and how your notice period lines up with that date.
  • Processing or activation fees: small charges such as 24 Hour Fitness's $4.99 activation fee or Crunch's processing fee.
  • Commitment vs month-to-month: a 12- or 13-month commitment often lowers the monthly price but may mean a buyout fee if you leave early.
  • Notice period: some chains need advance notice (EōS requires 30 days), so you may pay another month after you decide to quit.
  • Auto-renewal: some memberships renew automatically each year. Note the renewal date.
  • Freeze or hold: many clubs let you pause for a fee instead of cancelling (Life Time's on-hold rate is mostly $15 a month). Ask before you need it.

Can you cancel a gym membership online in 2026?

Sometimes. It depends on the chain, how you signed up and where you live. This section is general information, not legal advice. For a dispute, contact your state attorney general's consumer protection office or a lawyer licensed in your state.

What happened to the FTC click-to-cancel rule

The FTC's 2024 "click-to-cancel" amendments to its Negative Option Rule would have required cancellation to be as easy as sign-up. On 8 July 2025 the US Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit vacated the rule because the FTC had skipped a required preliminary regulatory analysis. In February 2026 the FTC formally restored the older 1973 rule text, and in March 2026 it published an advance notice of proposed rulemaking asking whether to try again. Comments closed on 13 April 2026. When we checked the Federal Register on 13 October 2026, the FTC had not published a new proposed rule. So there is no federal click-to-cancel rule in force today.

Federal protections that still apply

The Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act (ROSCA) still covers memberships sold online with automatic recurring charges. Sellers must clearly disclose the material terms, get your express informed consent before charging you, and provide "simple mechanisms for a consumer to stop recurring charges." The FTC also continues to bring cases under the FTC Act and ROSCA, including the LA Fitness case above.

State gym-contract laws

Many states have health-club laws. Life Time's annual report summarizes them as often requiring a right to cancel "in most cases, within three to 10 days after joining" with a refund of enrollment fees. Four examples we verified:

  • Texas (Occupations Code chapter 702): you can cancel for a full refund until midnight of the third business day after the contract date. The statute specifies certified mail for this notice, and the refund is due within 30 days. Contracts cannot exceed three years (five if financed). You are entitled to a partial refund if the gym closes without an alternative within 10 miles, moves more than 10 miles, or fails to provide advertised services, and on death or total and permanent disability. Moving away is not listed as a ground.
  • Washington (RCW 19.142): a three-business-day cancellation right, payments capped at 36 months, and cancellation for death or total disability. For contracts longer than one year, you can cancel for any reason with 30 days' written notice, and you can also cancel if you move more than 25 miles away. Refunds are due within 30 days.
  • California (Business and Professions Code §17602, for contracts from 1 July 2025): if you signed up online, the business must let you cancel online "at will," through a prominent link or button. Retention offers are allowed only if you can still cancel.
  • Georgia (O.C.G.A. §10-1-393.2): according to the Georgia Attorney General's consumer protection division, you have seven business days to cancel a health spa contract in writing, contracts are limited to 36 months, and the gym may keep no more than $100 for services you used. You can also cancel within 30 days of learning of a substantial change in services.

Other states have their own rules. Search for your state attorney general's page on health clubs or automatic renewals.

Cancellation checklist

  1. Before you sign, ask how to cancel (online, in person or by mail), the notice period and any buyout fee, and get the answer in your contract.
  2. Note your annual fee date and any auto-renewal date.
  3. Cancel in writing where possible. Keep the confirmation number, email or certified-mail receipt.
  4. Check your next two statements. If you are charged after cancelling, contact the gym, then your bank or card issuer.
  5. Report problems to your state attorney general or the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov.

How to choose a gym: a checklist

  1. Location and hours: pick a gym close to home or work, open when you will actually train. Pay for 24/7 access only if you will use it.
  2. Total first-year cost: enrollment + 12 months of dues + annual fee + taxes and processing fees.
  3. Trial pass first: use a free pass (24 Hour Fitness offers 5 days, for example) before you commit.
  4. Peak hours: visit at the exact time you plan to train. Count the free squat racks, benches and treadmills.
  5. Contract terms: month-to-month or commitment, notice period, buyout fee and cancellation method, all in writing.
  6. Hygiene: look for wipes or spray stations, clean locker rooms and showers, and staff who clean equipment during the day.
  7. Equipment for your goal: if you want to get stronger, you need racks, barbells and plates to apply progressive overload. If you need accountability, check the class schedule.
  8. Trainer credentials: see below.
  9. Extras that matter to you: pool, childcare, sauna, towels and access to other clubs when you travel.

Once you have joined, a simple full-body gym workout is an easy way to learn the equipment. If you are coming back after time off, see our plan for your first four weeks back at the gym.

Check the trainer's certification

A trainer's certification is the easiest credential to check. Look for a certification accredited by the National Commission for Certifying Agencies (NCCA), which sets standards for certification programs. NASM and ACE state on their own sites that their personal trainer certifications are NCCA-accredited. The US Department of Labor's O*NET certification finder lists the ACSM Certified Personal Trainer and the NSCA Certified Personal Trainer as NCCA-accredited. Ask which certification a trainer holds, confirm it with the certifying body, and check that their CPR/AED certification is current.

How we score these gyms

Each chain gets a score from 1 to 5 on five criteria, in half-point steps. The overall score is the average of the five, rounded to one decimal place. We score only what the chains publish and what the sources cited in this guide report, and we checked it all on 13 October 2026. We did not visit any of these gyms or buy a membership, and no chain paid for its score.

When a chain doesn't publish its prices, its value score is capped at 3.0, because you can't judge value without a price. Most chains set prices, hours and terms club by club, so your local club may score differently. These scores are a starting point for your shortlist. They are not a substitute for a trial visit at the club you'd join.

  • Value: the price compared with what's included, after enrollment, annual and processing fees.
  • Facilities: the equipment and amenities described, such as pools, courts, saunas, recovery areas and studios.
  • Access: typical hours (24/7 scores highest), day passes or free trials, and the number of locations.
  • Coaching: the range of classes, coaching and programming on offer.
  • Transparency: whether prices, contract and cancellation terms, and hours are clearly published.

FAQ

What is the cheapest gym chain in the US?

By advertised price, Planet Fitness ($15 Classic), Crunch (Base from about $9.99 with a 12-month commitment, per NerdWallet) and EōS ("as low as" $9.99 at select clubs) are the cheapest national chains. Once you add enrollment and annual fees, Planet Fitness's first year comes to about $229 to $288 at standard rates, while the cheapest EōS offer can reach about $380. Compare the first-year total at the club you would join.

What is a gym annual fee and when is it charged?

It is a yearly charge on top of monthly dues, often described as a maintenance or enhancement fee. Among the chains above it ranges from $49 (Planet Fitness) to $89 (Crunch). The billing date varies by chain and club, so ask when it is charged and whether cancelling before that date avoids it.

Is the FTC click-to-cancel rule in effect?

No. A federal appeals court vacated it in July 2025. The FTC began a new rulemaking process in March 2026 but had not proposed a new rule as of 13 October 2026. ROSCA and state laws still apply.

Can a gym make me cancel in person or by mail?

It depends on your state and how you joined. In California, if you signed up online for a contract from 1 July 2025 onward, the business must let you cancel online. Texas law specifies certified mail for cancelling within the three-day window. Federal law (ROSCA) requires a simple way to stop recurring charges for memberships sold online. Your contract should spell out the method, so read it before you sign.

Can I cancel a gym membership if I move?

Sometimes. Washington lets you cancel a contract longer than one year if you move more than 25 miles away. Texas does not list moving as a ground, though it does provide refunds if the gym closes or moves more than 10 miles. Many chains have their own relocation policies, so check your agreement.

Which gym chains are open 24 hours?

Anytime Fitness advertises 24/7 access for members, and the EōS clubs we checked in Houston are open 24/7. Do not assume from a chain's name: hours are set club by club, including at 24 Hour Fitness, so check the hours of the specific club you plan to join.

How much does a gym membership cost per month on average?

The Health & Fitness Association put average monthly dues at US fitness facilities at $65 in 2023, as cited by NerdWallet. Budget chains advertise far less, and premium clubs charge several times more.
